01
Trap Bar Deadlift
Main lift · heaviest thing you do today
160 lb
5 × 3
RPE 6–7
Rest 90–120s
Tempo controlled
Load the bar
Build up first
Empty × 1080 × 5115 × 3
Why it earns its place: the hinge pattern loads glutes and hamstrings, which is where seated cycling power is weakest. Trap bar over straight bar because the neutral grip and centred load are far kinder to your lower back.
Cues
  • Neutral spine, chest proud, ribs down
  • Drive through the heels, not the toes
  • Hip hinge, not a squat. Push the hips back to reach the handles
  • Brace before every rep, as if about to be punched
Watch for
  • Lower back rounding as you set up. If you cannot reach the handles flat-backed, raise the bar on blocks
  • Squatting it: knees shoved forward, hips too low. The shins stay near vertical
  • Yanking off the floor. Take the slack out of the bar first, then drive
  • Leaning back and hyperextending at the top. Stand tall and stop
  • Heels lifting or weight shifting onto the toes

02
Back Squat
Main lift · standard 45 lb barbell
115 lb
4 × 5
RPE 6–7
Rest 90s
Depth below parallel
Load the bar
Build up first
Empty × 1060 × 5
Why it earns its place: builds the quad strength and knee-extension force that a hinge alone does not, and the depth requirement keeps hip mobility honest after years in a fixed riding position.
Cues
  • Chest up, big breath into the belly, brace
  • Knees track out over the toes, never inward
  • Hip crease below the kneecap. That is the depth target
  • Whole foot planted, drive the floor away
Watch for
  • Knees caving in on the way up. Stop the set. Lighter load, cue knees out
  • Heels lifting. Usually ankle mobility. Try a small heel raise or lifting shoes
  • Pelvis tucking under at the bottom. Stop one inch above where it starts
  • Hips rising first and turning it into a good morning
  • Bar creeping too high onto the neck. It sits on the shelf of the rear delts

03
Weighted Pull-up
Accessory · dip belt or dumbbell between the feet
+5 lb over bodyweight
4 × 5–8
Rest 90s
Range full
Chin over bar
Why it earns its place: the pull keeps shoulders and upper back balanced against hours in a forward riding position, and gives you something to hold onto when climbing out of the saddle.
Cues
  • Dead hang at the bottom, arms straight
  • Pull the shoulder blades down and back before the elbows bend
  • Chest towards the bar, chin clears it
  • Lower under control, do not drop
Watch for
  • Kipping or swinging to generate momentum. If you need it, the load is too heavy
  • Half reps that never reach a straight-arm hang
  • Shrugging up into the ears instead of setting the scapulae down
  • Craning the chin over the bar while the chest stays low

04
Hanging Leg Raise
Accessory · ab wheel rollout is the alternative
Bodyweight
3 × 8–12
Rest 60s
Tempo slow, controlled
Why it earns its place: a braced trunk is what stops power leaking sideways when you stand up and throw the bike. This is the cheapest transfer in the session.
Cues
  • Start from a still hang. No swing
  • Tilt the pelvis back first, then lift the legs
  • Lower slower than you raised
  • Ribs down, lower back flat, not arched
Watch for
  • Swinging between reps and riding the momentum
  • Dropping the legs instead of controlling them down
  • Arching the lower back at the bottom, which turns it into a hip flexor exercise
  • Only bending the knees while the pelvis never moves
