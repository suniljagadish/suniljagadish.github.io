01
Trap Bar Deadlift
Main lift, volume version
150 lb
3 × 5
RPE 6-7
Rest 120s
Lighter than Session A, more reps
Load the bar
Build up first
Empty x 1085 x 5115 x 3
Why it earns its place: The hinge loads glutes and hamstrings, which is where seated cycling power is weakest. Trap bar over straight bar because the neutral grip and centred load are far kinder to the lower back.
Cues
  • Neutral spine, chest proud, ribs down
  • Drive through the heels, not the toes
  • Hip hinge, not a squat. Push the hips back to reach the handles
  • Brace before every rep, as if about to be punched
Watch for
  • Lower back rounding on set-up. If you cannot reach the handles flat-backed, raise the bar on blocks
  • Squatting it: knees shoved forward, hips too low. The shins stay near vertical
  • Yanking off the floor. Take the slack out of the bar first, then drive
  • Leaning back and hyperextending at the top. Stand tall and stop
  • Heels lifting or weight shifting onto the toes

02
Bulgarian Split Squat
Single leg, rear foot on a bench
2 x 25 lb dumbbells
3 × 8 each leg
RPE 6-7
Rest 90s
Weaker leg first
Why it earns its place: Single-leg strength and hip stability. Cycling hides left-right imbalance because the cranks force symmetry. This finds it.
Cues
  • Rear foot laces down on the bench, front foot far enough forward that the shin stays near vertical
  • Torso a touch forward, not folded
  • Drive through the front heel. The back leg is a kickstand, not a motor
  • Lower under control, pause, then up
Watch for
  • Front foot too close to the bench, so the knee crashes forward past the toes
  • Pushing off the back foot to help. That is a lunge, not a split squat
  • Front knee caving inward under load
  • Stance too narrow side to side, so all the attention goes on balance instead of the legs
  • Rushing the descent
Watch technique

03
Single-leg RDL
Single leg hinge
30 lb, one dumbbell
3 × 8 each leg
Rest 90s
Opposite hand to standing leg
Why it earns its place: Hamstring and glute strength on one leg, plus anti-rotation through the trunk. This is the movement that exposes and then fixes pedalling asymmetry.
Cues
  • Hinge at the hip, not the waist. The back leg and torso move as one line
  • Keep the hips square to the floor
  • Dumbbell in the hand opposite the standing leg
  • Fingertip on a wall while learning. Balance is not the point
Watch for
  • Hips opening and rotating outward on the way down. That is the main one
  • Rounding the back to reach lower
  • Bending the standing knee so much it becomes a one-legged squat
  • Chasing depth before owning the pattern
  • Rushing and using momentum to stand back up

04
Nordic Curl
Eccentric hamstring, do not go to failure
Body weight, assisted
3 × 5
Rest 90s
3 to 5 second lower
Why it earns its place: Eccentric hamstring strength is the single best protection against hamstring injury, and it is a genuine weak link for cyclists who never lengthen the hamstring under load.
Cues
  • Glutes squeezed hard the whole time
  • Hips stay extended. Do not hinge backwards
  • Lower over three to five seconds
  • Press lightly with the hands to get back up. Use only as much as you need
Watch for
  • Hinging at the hips instead of holding a straight line from knee to shoulder
  • Dropping fast because control has run out. Shorten the range instead
  • Taking it to failure. Stop while the rep still looks like a rep
  • Starting with no hands ready to catch you. Set up so you can bail safely

05
Bent-over Row
Upper back
100 lb
4 × 8
RPE 6-7
Rest 90s
Pull to the lower ribs
Load the bar
Build up first
Empty x 1060 x 5
Why it earns its place: Balances the pushing and forward flexion of riding. A stronger upper back is also what stops position collapsing late in a long event.
Cues
  • Hinge to roughly 45 degrees, flat back, brace
  • Set the shoulder blades back before the elbows bend
  • Pull to the lower ribs, not the chest
  • Lower under control to a full stretch
Watch for
  • Standing up as you pull, using the hips to throw the bar
  • Rounding the lower back once it gets heavy
  • Letting the bar drift away from the body
  • Yanking with the biceps and never engaging the back
  • Torso bobbing up and down every rep

06
Box Jump
Power, quality over height
20 in box
4 × 5
Rest 60s
Step down every rep
Why it earns its place: Rate of force development. This is the quality behind a standing acceleration and a sprint, and the one thing here that trains speed rather than strength.
Cues
  • Land softly and quietly
  • Land in roughly the same shape you took off in, not a deep tuck
  • Step down between reps. Never jump down
  • Every rep should look identical. Stop when they stop looking the same
Watch for
  • Chasing box height and landing in a deep squat. That is hip mobility, not power
  • Jumping down off the box. Pointless Achilles and knee risk
  • Landing stiff-legged with a thud
  • Grinding out fatigue reps. This is a power exercise, so quality is the whole point
Watch technique
